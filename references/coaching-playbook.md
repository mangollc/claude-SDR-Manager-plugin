# Coaching playbook

Each rep gets one focus: the first of five steps that's behind target. Fixing an early step often lifts everything after it.

## Language rules

- Treat every cause as a question. The numbers show where, not why.
- Start with what's working.
- Coach one thing at a time.
- Describe behavior, never character. Say "calls ended before an ask", not "lacks confidence".
- Never compare a rep with teammates.
- Never suggest hiring, firing, pay, or formal performance action. Offer the facts and suggest involving HR.
- Agree a small, specific action and a date to check it.

## Step 1: Activity

The rep isn't reaching enough people.

**Causes to explore**
- List building or admin is eating selling time
- Calling time isn't protected
- The rep is avoiding one channel, often the phone
- Touches aren't being logged, so the work is invisible
- Time off or tool problems

**Check first**
- Days active: is this a few missing days, or a low pace every day?
- Is every channel low, or only one?
- Did the drop start in a particular week?

**Practice ideas**
- Protect two calling blocks a day
- Build tomorrow's list before finishing today
- Run a team calling hour

**Questions**
- Walk me through a typical day last week. Where did the time go?
- Which part of outreach do you find yourself putting off?
- What would need to change to protect two calling blocks a day?

## Step 2: Response

The work is happening, but prospects aren't responding. Coach on the weakest metric named in the focus.

### Connect rate

**Causes to explore:** old or wrong phone numbers, calling when prospects are busy, too few direct or mobile numbers, or numbers flagged as spam.

**Practice ideas:** test different calling times, and use the best-verified numbers first.

**Questions**
- Where did this list come from?
- When do you get the most pickups?
- How many of these numbers turn out to be wrong?

### Email reply rate

**Causes to explore:** the wrong audience, a generic message, a long email, or emails landing in spam.

**Check first:** the bounce rate. If there's a deliverability warning, fix the list before coaching on the writing.

**Practice ideas:** review a handful of sent emails together, rewrite the opening line around the prospect's world, and make the ask smaller.

**Questions**
- Which emails got replies, and what did they have in common?
- Why would this person care about this message today?
- Could you cut this email in half?

### LinkedIn acceptance rate

**Causes to explore:** the wrong audience, an incomplete profile, or a connection note that reads like a pitch.

**Practice ideas:** tidy the profile, and send a short note with a real reason to connect.

**Questions**
- What would make you accept this request?
- What does your profile say to a buyer?

### LinkedIn reply rate

**Causes to explore:** pitching straight after connecting, or messages that aren't personal.

**Practice ideas:** open with something specific to the prospect, and ask a question rather than pitch.

**Questions**
- What was the first thing you said after they connected?
- Which messages started real conversations?

## Step 3: Meetings booked

People are responding, but conversations aren't becoming meetings.

**Causes to explore**
- The opener doesn't earn the next minute
- The same objection keeps ending calls
- The rep talks about the product rather than the prospect's problem
- The rep doesn't ask for the meeting clearly

**Check first**
- Listen to a few recent calls
- Note where conversations end
- List the three most common objections

**Practice ideas**
- Role-play the first thirty seconds
- Drill the top objections until the answers feel natural
- Practice a clear ask that offers two specific times

**Questions**
- Where do your conversations usually end?
- Which objection do you hear most, and what do you say?
- How do you ask for the meeting? Show me.

## Step 4: Meetings held

Meetings get booked, but prospects don't show up.

**Causes to explore**
- The prospect agreed to end the call, without real interest
- The meeting is booked too far ahead
- No invite or reminder went out
- The prospect doesn't know why the meeting matters

**Check first**
- How far ahead are meetings booked?
- Do no-shows share a source or type of prospect?

**Practice ideas**
- Send the invite during the call
- Confirm a day before, with one useful reason to attend
- Book within a few working days where possible
- Ask a qualifying question before booking

**Questions**
- For your last few no-shows, what made them say yes?
- What happens between booking and the meeting?
- How do you check that someone really wants to meet?

## Step 5: Meetings accepted

Meetings happen, but AEs don't accept them. Booked meetings can look great while wasting everyone's time.

**Causes to explore**
- The ideal customer isn't clear to the rep
- The rep books to hit a number
- The AE's standards aren't written down
- Handoff notes are thin

**Check first**
- Why were the last few meetings rejected?
- Is there a written definition of an accepted meeting?

**Practice ideas**
- Review rejected meetings with an AE
- Agree a short checklist to use before booking
- Write handoff notes that explain why this prospect, and why now

**Questions**
- What does a great meeting look like to your AE?
- Which of your recent meetings would you not have booked, looking back?
- What do you find out before you book?

## Other situations

**On track with a stretch focus:** start by celebrating it. Explore what's working, then choose one thing to push further. Ask whether the rep wants a bigger challenge or to help a teammate.

**On track with no focus:** ask what the rep wants to learn next.

**Onboarding:** focus on learning, not numbers. Ask what's confusing, what's going well, and what support would help.

**Review:** no step explains the gap. Check for meetings booked but not yet held, or a recent slow patch. Look again next week before drawing conclusions.

**Limited data:** say what's missing and ask for the file. Don't guess.

**Deliverability warning:** a high bounce rate suggests bad email addresses. Clean the list first, because poor delivery can hurt the whole team's sending.

**Team pattern:** if several reps share a focus, look at what they share: the list, the messaging, the tools, or the target. Fix that before coaching individuals.
