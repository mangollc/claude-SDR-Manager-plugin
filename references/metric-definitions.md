# Metric definitions

Use this to explain any number in plain language.

## The team

- **SDR:** a sales development rep, who makes first contact with potential customers and books meetings.
- **AE:** an account executive, who runs those meetings and closes deals.
- **Touch:** one outreach attempt, such as a dial, email, or LinkedIn message.
- **Cadence:** a timed series of touches across channels for one prospect.

## Time

Weeks run Monday to Sunday. Each scorecard shows this week and the last four weeks. Status and coaching focus use the four weeks, because one week has too few meetings to judge fairly.

Working days are Monday to Friday, minus holidays and the rep's time off.

## Activity

| Metric | Meaning | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Touches | Dials, emails, and LinkedIn touches added together | Shows whether enough outreach is happening |
| Days active | Working days with at least one touch | Separates steady work from a few big days |

Activity targets describe normal workload. Pushing volume higher isn't the goal.

## Response

| Metric | Calculation | What a low number usually points to |
|---|---|---|
| Connect rate | Connected calls ÷ dials | Prospect list quality or calling times |
| Email reply rate | Replies ÷ emails sent | Targeting or message relevance |
| Email bounce rate | Bounces ÷ emails sent | Bad email addresses, which can harm delivery for everyone |
| LinkedIn acceptance rate | Accepted requests ÷ requests sent | Targeting, profile, or connection note |
| LinkedIn reply rate | Replies ÷ messages sent | Message relevance |

## Meetings

| Metric | Calculation | What a low number usually points to |
|---|---|---|
| Conversations | Connected calls + email replies + LinkedIn replies | Not a rate. It's the base for meeting rate |
| Meeting rate | Meetings booked ÷ conversations | Talk track or objection handling |
| Meetings booked | Meetings with a booked date in the period | The headline count, though it can reward low-quality meetings |
| Meetings held | Held meetings with a due date in the period | The real goal |
| Show rate | Held ÷ meetings due | Weak qualification or no confirmation |
| Acceptance rate | Accepted ÷ held meetings reviewed | Booking the wrong prospects |

Meetings due are held, no-show, and cancelled meetings. Rescheduled and future meetings are left out until they have a final status. Meetings the AE hasn't reviewed are shown as pending.

## Statuses

| Step status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| On target | At or above target |
| Near | From 85% to under 100% of target |
| Below | Under 85% of target |
| Not enough data | Too small a sample to judge fairly |
| Not available | The data wasn't supplied |

| Rep status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| On track | Meeting goal met, and meetings are being accepted |
| Watch | A step is slightly behind, and none is far behind |
| Needs attention | At least one step is well behind |
| Review | Short of the goal, but no step explains why |
| Onboarding | In the first 30 days, so numbers aren't judged |
| Limited data | Not enough information, usually because a file is missing |

## Targets

Starting targets are published figures, listed with their sources on the report's final page.

- Response-rate targets are averages, so on target means typical, not excellent.
- The meeting rate target is calculated so the funnel adds up to the meeting goal. It's higher than published figures for calls alone, and this step may appear often until a team sets its own numbers.
- The acceptance target comes from dividing two Bridge Group median quotas.
- Only 60% of SDRs reached quota in the Bridge Group study, so a normal team will have some reps below the meeting target.
- New hires have lower count targets: none in their first 30 days, then 50%, 75%, and full targets from month 4.

A team's own numbers are always better than these.
